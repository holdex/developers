## Problem

Code that passes its tests and agent review can still do the wrong thing for its
users. On a system with real users, money, or sensitive data, that mistake
damages the client or Holdex.

## Solution

The human check is on the result, not the diff. Try the feature the way its
users will, and on high-stakes systems let users try it before it is switched on
for everyone.

1. Try the feature yourself on the preview, or on the deployed environment once
   the whole stack has merged.
1. Treat a system as high stakes when it handles real users, finances, or
   sensitive data, where a bug can cause reputational or material damage to the
   client or to Holdex.
1. On a high-stakes system, ship the feature behind a feature flag: merge the
   stack with the flag off, have users test the feature, then turn the flag on.
1. Leave the decision to turn the flag on to the Goal owner, a Holdex partner
   per [DEV-110](./DEV-110.md), together with the stakeholders. Releasing is a
   product decision, not a quality check, and the developer who merged does not
   make it alone.
1. Test a high-stakes feature thoroughly, through every job story the Spec
   names, not with a quick click-through.

### Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] The developer has tried the feature on a preview or after the stack merged
- [ ] A high-stakes feature ships behind a feature flag that stays off until
      users have tested it
- [ ] A high-stakes feature is tested through every job story in its Spec
- [ ] The Goal owner, with the stakeholders, decides when a high-stakes flag is
      turned on
