## Problem

A contributor works without a capable AI agent, or owns one and cannot get
results from it, so mechanical work still takes human hours and the cost of
every change stays at pre-AI levels.

## Solution

[DEV-440](./DEV-440.md) sets the standard your output is judged against: value
against cost. Meeting that standard is not possible on unaided human speed, so a
paid AI agent and the skill to drive it are conditions of contributing, not
optional upgrades.

1. Hold a paid plan on a current coding agent before you take work. The free
   tiers run out mid-task and are not a basis for planning. You bear the cost
   yourself; see [Expenses](../EXPENSES.md) for why.
1. Treat proficiency as your responsibility. Two people on the same plan can
   exhaust it in a day or stay inside the normal monthly range, and the
   difference is technique. Running out is a signal to change how you prompt and
   scope, not a reason to ask for a larger allowance.
1. Reach for the agent first on anything mechanical, and keep your own hours for
   the judgment, design, and domain knowledge it cannot supply.
1. When a task defeats the agent, say so in the Problem or the PR while it is
   happening. Silence followed by a large time submission is what
   [DEV-440](./DEV-440.md) rejects.

Good: a contributor hits a linter they cannot clear, spends fifteen minutes with
the agent, and flags the blocker in the PR. Bad: the same contributor spends
five hours on it by hand and reports the hours as the work.

### Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] The contributor holds a paid plan on a current AI coding agent
- [ ] Mechanical work is delegated to the agent rather than done by hand
- [ ] Credit exhaustion is answered with a change of technique, not a request
      for reimbursement or a larger allowance
- [ ] A task the agent cannot complete is flagged while it is blocking, not
      explained afterwards in a time submission
