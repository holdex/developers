## Problem

Feedback phrased as a verdict on a person puts them on the defence, so the
discussion turns to who was wrong instead of what to change.

## Solution

Be firm on the position and aim it at the object. A reader can act on "this
query runs once per row"; "you wrote a slow query" only asks them to defend
themselves. Softening the objection is not the fix either: an objective problem
still gets Request Changes per [DEV-410](./DEV-410.md).

1. Name the thing that is wrong: the line, the requirement, the behavior or the
   result.
1. Describe a third party (a vendor, a previous team, a client) by what happens
   in practice, not by a judgement of them.
1. Pair the problem with the fix or the ask, as [DEV-230](./DEV-230.md) requires
   of any message.
1. Say it once. Repeated negations read as an accusation nobody made.

```md
Good: This step lets the initiator approve their own change. The approver
      picker should exclude the initiator.
Bad:  You didn't think about self-approval at all. This is not how approvals
      work, and it's not what we agreed.
```

### Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Criticism names the work it is about, not the person who produced it
- [ ] Third parties are described by what happens, without a verdict
- [ ] Each objection comes with the fix or the ask
