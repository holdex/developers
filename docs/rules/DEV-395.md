## Problem

A vendor is connected using a member's personal email or card, so the company
does not own it. Some accounts have no transfer path at all, so the only fix at
offboarding is rotating every credential from scratch.

## Solution

Ownership of a vendor account is set once, at creation, not fixed later. Before
a PR wires up a new vendor or third-party tool:

1. Create the account under a Holdex-owned email and, where the vendor supports
   it, a Holdex organization or team, not a personal account.
1. Attach billing to a Holdex payment method. A personal card may sit as a
   fallback only, never as the primary method.
1. If the vendor has no concept of transferable ownership (an app registered
   under a personal developer account, for example), treat the underlying
   platform account itself as company-owned before the integration is built on
   top of it.

A reviewer checks vendor ownership the same way they check any other PR
requirement: a PR that introduces a new vendor integration does not merge until
the account it depends on is confirmed Holdex-owned.

This rule closes the gap two incidents exposed: billing for Vercel and
Trigger.dev sitting on a personal account, and a Telegram bot with no transfer
path at all once its owner left.

### Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Every vendor account a PR's integration depends on is created under a
      Holdex-owned email and, where the vendor supports it, a Holdex
      organization or team
- [ ] Billing for the account is attached to a Holdex payment method, with any
  personal card only as a fallback
- [ ] A reviewer confirms account ownership before approving a PR that
  introduces a new vendor or third-party tool
