## Problem

A message that opens with a greeting, praise or a personal aside makes the
reader dig for the point, and one that never states its ask leaves them unsure
what to do.

## Solution

The reader should know from the first line what a message decides, answers or
asks. Anything that does not carry the work slows them down on the way to it.
This is "Keep it Simple" from the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md),
applied to every issue, PR description, comment, review reply and chat message.

1. Put the decision, answer, status or question in the first line.
1. Keep to the work: topics, deliverables, questions, status, decisions and
   outcomes. Leave out greetings, compliments, flattery, and remarks about your
   own hesitation or worry.
1. Cut openers that announce a point instead of making it, such as "Worth
   noting", "Just to add" or "Good question".
1. End with what you need, from whom. When nothing is needed, stop.

```md
Good: Blocked on the MAS field list. @owner, which company fields does MAS
      receive? I need it to finish the edit gate.
Bad:  Hi @owner, hope you're well! Great work on the spec so far. I've been
      looking into the edit gate and I'm a bit worried about the fields...
```

### Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] The first line carries the decision, answer, status or question
- [ ] The message holds no greeting, flattery or personal aside
- [ ] A message that needs something from someone names what and from whom
